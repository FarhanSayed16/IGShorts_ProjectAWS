# IGshorts — Where a local creator stands

One decision. Not built yet. The India and Global charts stay as they are.

---

## Decision

A person searches an Instagram handle and sees an **audience group**. Not a live follower total. Not a worldwide rank.

| Group | Rule |
|---|---|
| Community | 10 thousand to under 100 thousand |
| Established | 100 thousand to under 1 million |
| Public | 1 million to under 10 million |
| Major | 10 million and above |

Under 10 thousand, or no reliable count: **Not grouped yet.**

The first saved reading goes in the group that contains it. 105 thousand is Established. 100 thousand and 101 thousand are both Established.

On a later reading the label stays put unless the new count is clearly inside the next group. That margin starts at about 15% and is a setting we can change. It is not applied to the first reading.

---

## What they see

A ladder of the four groups, with their group filled in.

A state, instead of a live badge:

- **Recently checked** — inside the freshness window we set
- **Update overdue** — the saved reading is older than that window
- **Not measured yet** — no reliable count
- **Could not verify** — the lookup returned nothing usable

The exact count, the source, the time, and the previous group stay in our records. The page shows the group and the state. A failed lookup does not erase the last good reading, and it is never stored as zero.

If they are also on the India or Global chart, that chart still shows their place. This lookup does not replace it.

---

## Search and tracking are separate

**Search.** The visitor enters an exact handle. We check saved records first. If an approved source can resolve it, we try a fresh lookup. An unknown or unsupported account gets a clear message, not a guess.

**Tracking.** Only a successfully measured account is saved for the weekly refresh, or someone explicitly chooses “Track this creator.” A random search does not become a permanent record by itself.

---

## What we do next

1. Build the four groups and the ladder for people we already have. Nothing new to buy.
2. Test the boundaries before launch, including a first count of 105 thousand and a later count just over a line.
3. Prove Meta on its own, before promising lookup of new handles. Five checks: a professional account we control, a public creator we do not control, a personal account, which fields and limits come back, and which permissions review will actually grant.
4. If the public-creator check fails, keep the product ready for another legitimate source. Do not promise a blue-tick search.
5. Add lookup of a new handle, with the last good count kept when a check fails.
6. Refresh tracked accounts about once a week. Move the label only when the new reading clears the margin.
7. A history line of the group only after two valid readings exist.

---

## What we have to get

Nothing for steps 1 and 2.

A new handle needs a source that can read one public professional account. Meta’s official lookup can do that for some accounts. It is a possible source, not a guaranteed one. An app, an account we own, and an access key do not open every creator. Personal accounts return nothing. A public product also needs Meta’s review.

| Get this, only if the Meta proof works | From |
|---|---|
| A developer app | Meta for Developers. We create it. |
| One Instagram professional account we own, linked to a Facebook Page | Our account. It asks. It is not the creator being searched. |
| Approval to look up other public professional accounts | Meta App Review, plus Advanced Access. |
| A long-lived access key | Meta, after approval. It expires and must be renewed. |
| The exact handle | The person searching, or an admin. |

We do not need their password, a purchased influencer list, or a new database.
